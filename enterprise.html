Enterprise experience

I've spent most of my career inside large, regulated financial institutions. I know what it takes to change them.

Bank of America (2010-2016)

I was recruited by the COO to fix process and technology gaps during the mortgage crisis. We had a million loans that needed modification under federal mandate and the systems couldn't keep up.

I learned the operation by sitting with the people doing the work, then unified 47 federated data sources so we could see what was happening behind over a million modification decisions. I built most of it myself, then hired the team to run it. The daily report was moving the work for 15,000 associates within months. The standard was 95% of loans worked within 30 days of entering the process against 70% actual, and closing that gap was the hard part. The OCC allowed Bank of America to keep servicing mortgages.

The class action alleged the workflow and its controls had not been done properly. I owned that workflow and had built the compliance control framework behind it. I was the primary data witness and the last deposed. It held, and the case was dismissed.

Wells Fargo (2019-2022)

Credit risk data infrastructure. Recruited to upgrade the data platform so credit risk could improve its automated decisioning. I shifted left on metadata and security until metadata became the control plane: every execution validated against it, lineage and impact known at full coverage, audit evidence from one mechanism instead of four.

Months between an idea and its execution became hours. 22,000+ transformations a day produced the offline model the automated decisions were made against. I built dbt-style transformation and orchestration out of SAS and shell because the commercial products were never going to be approved, recruited the team internally, and we had it running within weeks. Still in use years after I left.

GE and Six Sigma (2000-2017)

Six Sigma Master Black Belt. Applied statistics and design of experiments against live process data, hundreds of times over, at GE, Bank of America and Wells Fargo. This is where I learned to look at a system, find the constraint, and fix it systematically.
