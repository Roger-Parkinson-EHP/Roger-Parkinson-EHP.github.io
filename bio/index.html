The problem I work on

The most important unsolved problem in this field is how an enterprise feeds its intellectual capital to agents in the most consumable form. Models and harnesses are commodities; the context an organization has accumulated is not, and the question every organization has to ask is how much of it they are hiding from their agents behind an obstacle course of tool calls instead of a query.

My answer is a graph, and I measure it. Every conversation, pull request, doc and code change is captured continuously into a queryable, linked record: objects, links, actions and decision lineage, searchable by keyword and by meaning with a code graph beside it. An agent pulls a structured, bounded picture of the problem into context the same way after every reset. Every answer states its coverage and freshness, so the agent can tell what it has not seen. A fixed probe set scores retrieval before and after every change. Two numbers decide agent effectiveness: how long it takes to get the full picture, and the quality of the feedback loop from there.

Building now

  • CatalystFire, 2026

    An agentic platform built and run solo, locally. Anthropic and OpenAI agents work the same problem in parallel in isolated worktrees, every change lands as a pull request with its tests, and high stakes changes are reviewed by a different model family than the one that wrote them. Underneath is the context layer described above, on QMD and GitNexus, with a fixed probe set scoring every change.

  • AncestralFire, in beta

    Family history, told as stories. A family tree file becomes a graph inside each subscriber's own isolated agent; every ancestor is enriched from sources measured one by one (the Wikidata graph, period texts such as the 1911 Britannica, Europeana's national collections, the UK National Archives, historic newspapers, a gazetteer of places as they existed at the time); and the agent tells their story as a letter, a pedigree, a relationship graph or a migration map, always separating sourced fact from inference. Being rebuilt MCP first, so the same agent works inside Claude, ChatGPT and the web app. Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects, Vectorize and Workers AI.

  • VoiceCatalyst

    Speech into agents, about four times faster than typing, on local models, on the Mac and the iPhone. A wrong word becomes a wrong instruction, so the fix is data rather than acoustics: context harvested live and the person's own vocabulary and corrections are substituted in real time, lifting accuracy by up to 20 points in my benchmarks, most on smaller models.

How I work

  • Proof of concept first, then production. That is how the fund's platform was built and how the treasury platform shipped in three months.
  • I write the code. I learn the work at the level of the people doing it, build the first version myself, then build the team that runs it.
  • Context curated into a graph, so the agent queries once instead of scrambling through tool calls.
  • Measured, not asserted: probe sets before and after every change, and ablations that reproduce.
  • Evaluation, provenance and guardrails built in at the start. Bolted on at the end they are theater, and theater costs you your speed.
  • Stack: Python, TypeScript, Swift, C#, SQL; PostgreSQL, BigQuery, DuckDB; Terraform, Bazel, GitHub Actions; GCP, AWS, Cloudflare.

What I think about

The tools got better. The judgment didn't get cheaper. AI dropped the cost of iteration. It didn't drop the cost of being wrong; now you can be wrong at scale. Twenty five years of watching things break in production at banks, and a fund where wrong code lost real money overnight, is pattern recognition you can't prompt your way into.

DataOps is accounting for your data. Double entry bookkeeping recorded every transaction twice, and if the books didn't balance, something broke. Most data estates never built the equivalent of a chart of accounts. At Wells Fargo we did: metadata validation, lineage and automated reconciliation.

Books that shaped how I build: Goldratt's The Goal (find the one constraint; optimizing anything else is waste), Lencioni's The Advantage (a healthy team beats a smart one), Ridley's The Evolution of Everything (let systems evolve), and Pressfield's The War of Art (show up and do the work).
