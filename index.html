Roger Parkinson

I build financial systems and the agent platforms that work on them. The whole stack.

The short version

I grew up on a potato farm in Idaho. I joined military intelligence. I spent 12 years at Bank of America and sat in depositions defending data during the mortgage crisis. I built the credit risk data platform at Wells Fargo. Then I co-founded a quant fund with two partners and ran its technology for eight years.

This year I built a corporate treasury platform by myself in three months: double-entry ledger, payments through JPMorgan's API and Stripe, Plaid verification, reconciliation across all 23 ACH return codes. Then I spent the rest of the year on the problem underneath all of it: how an agent gets the context it needs to do real work.

I like building things. I like it a lot.

What I've built

Bank of America (2004–2016)

12 years, 5 SVP roles. Recruited into the mortgage crisis under a federal MRA. Unified 47 data sources so a million modification decisions could be seen and moved. The standard was 95% of loans worked within 30 days of entering the process; we were at 70%, and closing that gap was the work. I was the primary data witness in the class action, and it was dismissed.

Wells Fargo (2019–2022)

Credit risk data infrastructure. Metadata became the control plane: every execution validated against it, lineage and impact known at full coverage. Months between an idea and its execution became hours. 22,000+ transformations a day. The platform is still running years later.

Event Horizon Partners (2017–2026)

Co-founder and CTO of a systematic futures fund, now closed. Three people built the whole thing: streaming market data into BigQuery, a decision-science framework of continuous experimentation, champion versus challenger promotion, stability gates and walk-forward testing, and automated execution under NFA governance. Near-random data is the hardest test I know of whether a platform works.

Treasury platform (2025–2026)

Built from scratch in 3 months, solo. Double-entry ledger, 31 tables, multi-rail money movement through JPMorgan and Stripe, Plaid verification, approval workflows, affiliate distribution, reconciliation with auto-reversal across all 23 ACH return codes. One domain model served three ways: a front end for people, a workflow engine, and an MCP server plus CLI for agents. Terraform, GitHub Actions, three-environment promotion.

How I work now

Good models cannot compensate for bad context. I build and run my own agentic platform, locally: Anthropic and OpenAI agents work the same problem in parallel, every change lands as a pull request with its tests, and every conversation, pull request, doc and code change is captured continuously into a linked, queryable record with a code graph beside it. An agent pulls a bounded picture of the problem into context the same way every session, every answer says what it covers and how fresh it is, and a fixed probe set scores retrieval before and after every change. The most important unsolved problem in this field is how an enterprise feeds its intellectual capital to agents in the most consumable form. That is what I work on.
